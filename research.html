What I work on

Research

My research uses statistical models to connect the information observed during sporting contests to predictions about future outcomes and their uncertainty.

01 / Football analytics

Expected goals, team strength & league simulation

Shot-level event data provide information about the quality of scoring opportunities beyond observed goals. I study expected-goals models and how estimated scoring strengths can be translated into probabilistic forecasts of matches and league tables.

  • Estimate goal probabilities from shot characteristics.
  • Connect shot-level estimates to team scoring strengths and Poisson-based match simulation.
  • Use simulation and resampling to represent uncertainty in final league outcomes.

02 / Bayesian statistics

Football forecasting with ratings and recent performance

This research develops Bayesian hierarchical models for football goal counts and examines whether match-level indicators of recent form improve forecasts beyond established rating systems such as Elo.

  • Hierarchical goal models with team effects and rating-informed covariates.
  • Forecast evaluation with chronological or rolling-origin validation.
  • Proper scoring rules, uncertainty quantification and comparisons with simpler models.

03 / Probabilistic simulation

Ball-by-ball cricket win probabilities

In a run chase, the information available changes after every delivery. I am developing a generative framework to simulate the remaining innings and estimate the probability of a successful chase at each point in the match.

  • Ball-level models for runs, wickets and delivery outcomes.
  • Monte Carlo simulation of the remaining match.
  • Win-probability estimation as new information arrives, with chronological evaluation.

Research in progress. A dynamic latent-state extension is not claimed as an implemented result.
